Aeroblaster: find-it, fix-it repair for the Voliro T Voliro AG internship, with Monti Power

Jul – Dec 2025

Corrosion on silos, tanks and other tall steel structures usually means scaffolding or rope access and a shutdown, even for a small spot. The Voliro T drone could already find that corrosion through contact inspection. This project made it able to fix it on the same flight.

  • Led development of the blaster payload, built around Monti Power's Bristle Blaster head, from concept to a 3D-printed PLA prototype in one week, then through nylon and CNC-machined metal versions to bring it as close to production as possible.
  • Designed its core mechanical architecture, which the production Aeroblaster still uses after later iterations. The payload weighs under 800 g.
  • Moved the frame from printed plastic to aluminum so it doubles as the heat sink: the drive motor mounts directly to it, hidden behind the transmission cover.
  • Designed and prototyped a filtered cooling housing with a fan driven by the motor's own rotation. The fan creates negative pressure that pulls air in through filters, keeping fine metal dust from blasting out of the motor.
  • Designed the coating roller payload. The roller in use today is my version.
  • Together the two payloads complete the workflow on one platform: inspect, blast the spot to bare metal, roll on a protective coating, and log it all with photos and timestamps.
  • The payload preps steel to a profile comparable to SSPC-SP 11 (25 µm or more), validated by an independent lab and reviewed by SGK, the Swiss Society for Corrosion Protection.
  • On its first field job it repaired three 30 × 30 cm patches on a cement plant silo in under an hour, with no production downtime.

Now sold by Monti Power and Voliro as the Aeroblaster: montipower.com/pages/aeroblaster

Pants Pulleez: seated dressing aid ME 403 Access by Design, Cal Poly · team of three

Spring 2026

Built for a wheelchair user with muscular dystrophy who could get his pants to his knees with a grabber, but couldn't pull them over his hips while seated. Two cords clip to the back of the waistband and run through pulleys on a floor-to-ceiling tension pole beside the toilet. Pulling a handle down in front lifts one side of the pants at a time, and the pole installs with no drilling.

  • Did all of the CAD, and designed and FDM-printed every custom part: pulley holders, boom brackets, rope retainers and ball handles in PLA at 75% infill.
  • With a teammate, water-jet cut the post top plates, machined the lathe-turned adjustable-foot adapters and coated the aluminum frame.
  • Held a 50 lb load through the full system with no failure, twice the user's estimated 25 lb maximum pull.
  • Pulling down in front produced 73% more force than reaching back and pulling up (38 lb vs 22 lb), confirming the core idea of the design.
  • Came in at $99 in purchased parts against a $150 budget.
